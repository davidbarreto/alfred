# Day and week planning with Alfred

Alfred only gathers facts and stores what was agreed; you do the scheduling. Mechanics and field semantics: `docs/modules/planning.md`. The user's own routines, people and habits are **not** in this file: they come from the `preferences` of every `day_plan get` (wake/sleep, work target, office goal, `travel_times`, `conventions`) and from memory. If a rule you need is missing, ask once and offer to store it in `planning.conventions`.

Fixed commitments (pickups, classes, appointments), recurring tasks and activity targets are Alfred data and come back from `get`; don't ask the user to repeat them and don't hard-code them here.

Always look at the whole week, even when asked about one day, to check constraints and spread tasks.

## Flow (one round when possible)

0. **Weekly planning only: ask about work meetings first.** Ask whether all of the week's work meetings are in Alfred's calendar (they are easy to forget, and a plan without them is wrong) and wait for the answer, unless the user already said so or asked you to plan without asking; then say in the summary that meetings may be missing.
1. **One call for the data:** `alfred_day_plan get` with `date` = first day and `days` = days left through Sunday (max 7). The result already includes `saved_plan`; don't call `get_saved` for the same range. Read `preferences` and `warnings`.
2. **Weekly planning only:** load last week once (`get_saved`, or `review` for per-item planned/done/missed counts). Look for patterns (time of day, kind of item), not single misses. Skip when replanning mid-week.
3. **Holidays and trips:** `holidays` lists a day's public holidays as "Name (PT)", from the planning calendar only (the first `HOLIDAY_CALENDARS` entry); whether they are days off comes from `conventions` (ask once and offer to store it if missing). `trips` marks days the user is away or travelling: plan only around the trip's `agenda`, nothing at home, and finish before `leave_home_at` on the departure day. List recurring events on trip days and offer to `skip` them.
4. **Where the user is and how they travel:** an event's `location` (with `location_id`) is one of the user's frequent places. Each place has a fixed `kind` (home, work, school, gym, supermarket, ...: see `alfred_location`); find a place by kind (`alfred_location list --kind school`), never by guessing its name. Alfred derives no travel for them: you build each day's route. Decide the places in order from the events and `conventions` (going home between two places when there is time and a reason, e.g. a shower after the gym; none between two events at the same place; none for remote, free or all-day events, unless the user goes anyway), then look each leg up in `preferences.travel_times`, the map of every saved route, sent once per `get` (Home is `home`, other places their location name in lowercase, a route counts both ways unless the reverse is saved). Pick the fastest public transport, or walking when it is at most `walk_tolerance_minutes` longer, car only when it is the only option; when the car is much faster (about 15 min or more), ask "can you take the car for this one?". Leave-by = event start − leg minutes. Save every leg as a `transport` block titled "Home → Office (metro, 50)". A leg with no saved route is a guess of `default_travel_minutes`, not a fact: ask for the real minutes and offer to save them (`alfred_location update <place> --from <origin>:<mode>=<minutes>`); `warnings` list located places with no route from Home. Events with no location still come with derived travel blocks from their `travel_minutes`; one with `assumed: true` is the default, a question for the user (link a location, set `travel_minutes`, or mark it remote or free). A saved day with in-person located events and no `transport` legs is missing its route (Alfred never adds one): add the legs with `edit`.
4a. **Find conflicts before planning:** check every `cant_miss` and `important` event against fixed commitments and the travel it needs, including the legs you built in step 4: a leg that must start before the previous event ends, or that runs into a meeting, is a conflict (e.g. leaving the office in time for an afternoon call at home). `warnings` report overlaps only between what Alfred returns (events, routine steps, travel of unlocated events), never the legs you build, so report those yourself. Overlaps are never resolved by Alfred. Meetings can shift an hour in UTC around US/EU daylight-saving changes (late Oct / early Nov, March).
5. **Ask only about real conflicts** you can't solve from `preferences`: at most 3 questions, together with the proposal. Beyond that, protect `cant_miss` items and say what you chose. If the request is clear, plan and save without asking.
6. **Suggest meeting moves:** plan around meetings as they are, then look for moves that would save time or make a day easier: a meeting that forces a home day on an office day (or a commute for one call), one outside the hours in `conventions`, one that squeezes travel to a `cant_miss` commitment, a short gap between meetings that wastes a focus block, scattered meetings that could be clustered, or one on top of a habit when a free slot is nearby. Suggest at most 3, each with the new slot and the gain. Suggest only: others are in the meeting and `reschedule` writes through to Google, so move one with `alfred_event` `reschedule` only after the user confirms it moved (or owns it and asks), then replan the affected days.
7. **Errands:** collect what needs doing outside: the `shopping` section of `get` (pending and due counts, a suggested run, counts `by_category`), tasks with `context` "out", and anything running out. Use the category counts and the category-to-shop rules in `conventions` to decide which places are needed (supermarket, pharmacy, other shop), then find them with `alfred_location list --sells <category>` or `--kind` (supermarket, pharmacy, mall, variety_store, hardware_store, ...); call `alfred_shopping` list only when you place a stop and need the item names for its title. Place each stop next to where the user already is (on the way back from a fixed commitment, near the office on an office day, after the gym), never before a fixed appointment. Prefer a short stop at a close shop for a few urgent items and one bigger run for the rest; don't plan a dedicated trip for one or two items that can wait. Use `travel_times` for the shop; if the place is missing, ask once and offer to store it. Save a stop as an `other` block named after the place and its purpose, with a `transport` leg if it isn't on an existing route.
8. **Calendar changes:** don't change a recurring series for a one-off exception; use `alfred_event` `skip` / `reschedule` on the occurrence, or put the exception in the plan and its day notes. Create or delete events only when the office/work counts need them.
   - **Creating an office day:** `alfred_event` `add` with `title` "Office", `start` / `end` with UTC offset (arrival and leave-by from `conventions`; at least `office.min_minutes` long to count toward the goal), `tags` = the office and work tags (`day_plan settings`, default `office,work`), `presence` `in_person` (the default), and `location_id` = the location of kind `work`: look it up with `alfred_location list --kind work` and pass its id or name; never assume a name such as "Office". With several work locations, use the one the week's other office events link to, or ask; with none, ask the user and create it with `alfred_location add <name> --kind work --from home:<mode>=<minutes>`. No `travel_minutes` (only for events with no location); leave `availability` unset (blocked) and the free-text `location` empty (it is only for Google Calendar; Alfred fills it with the location's address).
   - **Office events stay blocked.** Work-tagged events (and their routine steps) entirely inside an in-person office event are not overlaps; anything else inside it (a personal appointment) is, on purpose. Never mark an office event `free` to silence warnings: a free event gets no commute. Clear a wrong `free` with `alfred_event` `update` `id` + `availability: ""`.
   - **Check it** in the next `get`: the event carries `location_id`, `location` (the location's name) and `availability: blocked`, `office.planned_this_week` went up, and the routes to it are in `preferences.travel_times`. Alfred adds no travel blocks for it: plan the legs as in step 4. An "Unknown location" error means the name is wrong: `alfred_location list`.
   - **Linking an existing event** (an office day with no `location_id`, which comes back with "Travel to X" blocks from its own `travel_minutes`): `alfred_event` `update` with `id` + `location_id` (its `travel_minutes` are then ignored); on a series it covers every occurrence. No `location_id` parameter on `alfred_event` means the tool schema is stale: restart the mcp container.
   - Locations are for frequent places only: a one-off appointment gets `travel_minutes` (ask) or nothing. When unlocated events with the same calendar location (or title) keep coming back with travel minutes, propose making that place a location with its travel times and linking the events.
9. **Save only the days that changed.** `save` takes `days` as a JSON string `[{"date","items","notes"}]`. Every item needs a UTC offset and must start on its date (local). Notes are short and explain the shape of the day. For a small change to a saved day, `edit` (replace blocks in `[replace_from, replace_to)`) keeps the other blocks and their flags.
10. **Reply briefly:** what changed and why, deadline/data problems (wrong deadline, task open after its deadline), meeting moves worth asking for (step 6), the errands you placed and where, and one next step if there is a real one. Don't restate the plan.

## Planning principles

- Deadline and overdue work tasks get the first slot of the morning.
- Anchor habits to things that already happen (meals, a commute, shutdown). Don't plan them as floating items.
- Give habits a sensible minimum length and a realistic weekly target; check `review` before inflating one.
- Batch errands next to wherever the user is already going, and after a fixed appointment, not before (step 7).
- Don't overload weekends. Keep late evenings free: items planned after the user's usual shutdown are the ones that get missed.
- Tasks with no estimate default to 30 min and are listed in `warnings`. For known long ones assume more, say so in the item note, and write the answer back with the task `update` action.
- Overlapping items are fine when one is passive (a podcast during a commute).
- Work target is per workday (`workdays` preference); weekends have none unless `work_minutes` is passed.

## Block kinds

- With `ref_id`: `event`, `task`, `study_item`, `activity`, `routine_step`, `routine_block`.
- Free-form, no `ref_id`: `work` (focused work that isn't a task; counts toward committed work), `transport` (every leg between places: commutes, trips to located events, and the commute of an office day that has no event yet; not the derived travel blocks of unlocated events), `meal`, `break`, `other`.

## Review loop

`miss` / `unmiss` / `done` / `undone` (by date + `ref_id`, or title) mark blocks of a saved version in place: no new version, the task stays open. A block with neither flag is unmarked, not known-done. When a miss pattern becomes a rule, propose adding it to `planning.conventions` so every client gets it.

## If a call is rejected as unknown

The mcp container fetches the command catalog once at startup. An unknown kind or action (`transport`, `work`, `meal`, `edit`, `review`, `miss`) means the tool schema is stale: restart the mcp container.
